# The Era of AI Pricing

**Published:** 2025-10-03  
**Source:** [https://bmak.substack.com/p/the-era-of-ai-pricing](https://bmak.substack.com/p/the-era-of-ai-pricing)

---

For years, SaaS pricing was based on a simple pricing model: price per seat. Easy to explain, easy to forecast, and easy to sell. But that model is breaking down. As AI reshapes workflows and automation reduces the need for “seats,” the link between user count and value delivered is eroding.

## **Why the Shift?**

The traditional seat-based model works in a world where value scales with headcount. More employees means more licenses, and more licenses means more revenue. But AI changes this equation. One AI agent can amplify or replace the work of many humans and one human can deploy many agents. Instead of growth in seats, companies are seeing flat or even declining user counts, while actual usage of the system skyrockets. This shift breaks the seat metric as a proxy for value.

## **Seats and Consumption**

As Madhavan Ramanujam highlights in a recent [podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NSYbyPdc1-A) with Manny Medina, for most companies the easier transition is a hybrid approach. Seat counts may flatten or even decline, but consumption often increases. That creates an opportunity to monetize usage through tokens. By attaching token allotments to plans, usage becomes a factor for plan selection as customers outgrow their allocations.

Tokens add a variable layer on top of a fixed base. More importantly, they give sales teams a natural excuse for an expansion conversation when customers exceed their allocation. Hybrid models preserve seat-based simplicity while unlocking upside from rising usage.

Still, token metering is just a proxy for value. It can work well at the platform level since a base model supports many different use cases. But for higher-in-the-stack applications that can tie their value to specific business outcomes, relying on token metering alone should ideally be avoided. For example, two different applications might be delivering the same value but one of them used double the tokens. Why should the end user be penalized by paying for more tokens in this case?

## **The Attribution Play**

The more radical path is to charge for the value created. Customers are better served by measuring outputs directly such as deals closed, revenue influenced, or tasks automated so pricing aligns more closely with the actual business impact delivered.

In this world attribution is everything. The closer a company can get to measuring its direct impact, the more viable it becomes to shift to outcome pricing. This is the gold standard of value-based pricing: incentives fully aligned between customer and vendor, with fees justified by ROI rather than cost to serve.

From the customer perspective, outcome-based pricing also feels fairer and more predictable. Instead of paying for logins or tokens, buyers know they are paying in proportion to results achieved. That alignment reduces friction and builds trust, even if vendors face more complexity in proving attribution.

However, attribution comes with many challenges:

- **Shared credit:** Multiple tools, processes, and people contribute to any given outcome, making it hard to isolate impact.
- **Measurement gaps:** Many organizations lack the instrumentation to track outcomes consistently and credibly.
- **Trust issues:** Customers may dispute whether outcomes are directly attributable to your product, especially in complex enterprise environments.

For example, how much credit should an AI assistant get for a closed deal when a human salesperson, CRM system, and marketing campaign all played roles? Without clarity, pricing conversations can stall or turn contentious. Companies pursuing outcome-based models must invest heavily in analytics, integrations, and transparent methodologies to make attribution defensible.

An interim step to perfect attribution (e.g., revenue booked), could be the price per action (e.g., price per lead generated or phone call made) model where the action is a proxy of value. Of course, this has obvious challenges as customers need to believe that the proxy is a good one so this could introduce friction on negotiations and not allow the vendor to optimize revenue as they can’t fully tie the final outcome to their actions.

## **What the Future looks like**

We have already entered an era where the traditional SaaS pricing models are challenged to price the new AI products so the shift is clear. While this might seem like a big shift, the fundamentals remain the same.

Pricing should scale with value delivered. You can claim a percentage of the value provided to the customer. Avoid associating your pricing with costs as they are irrelevant (and change fast as new tools and platforms emerge). Exciting times.
