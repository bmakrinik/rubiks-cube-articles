Trying a new format with thoughts on current topics and I couldn’t start from anything other than ChatGPT and its impact. For the first time, the engineering profession feels some competition and this is self imposed.

**Productivity Gains**

A common topic of discussion about ChatGPT productivity gains is how much more productive GPT makes a profession. While estimates vary, it is widely accepted that the impact is significant. For engineering, GitHub conducted a [study](https://github.blog/2022-09-07-research-quantifying-github-copilots-impact-on-developer-productivity-and-happiness/) where engineers using Copilot completed a task with a higher success rate (78% vs. 70% for control) and of those who completed the task, Copilot-assisted engineers completed it 55% faster (1h 11 mins vs. 2h 41 mins; p=0.0017). Similarly, AWS [claims](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/announcing-new-tools-for-building-with-generative-ai-on-aws/) that “participants who used CodeWhisperer completed tasks 57% faster, on average, and were 27% more likely to complete them successfully than those who didn’t use CodeWhisperer.” These results may not be representative of an average engineer, but one can see how we get to 20-30% productivity gains in a few years.

A rarely discussed second order effect is that by improving productivity, we also cut down on communication overhead. If you have worked in a company larger than a few hundred people this will probably resonate. I asked ChatGPT to help me quantify this one.

> *Communication overhead is the amount of time and effort required to communicate and coordinate among team members. It increases as the team size grows, because the number of possible communication channels or individual interactions between team members also grows. One way to calculate the communication overhead is by using this formula:*
>
> *Communication Overhead = Team Size \* (Team Size – 1) / 2*
>
> *For example, a team of 5 people has 10 communication channels, a team of 10 people has 45 communication channels, and a team of 15 people has 105 communication channels.*
>
> *As you can see, the communication overhead increases rapidly as the team size increases. This means that more time and effort is spent on communicating and coordinating rather than on doing the actual work. This can reduce the productivity and efficiency of the team.*

Of course, this is a simplification as not all people have to talk to each other, but take for example a product manager at a big tech company that tries to launch a new product. They have to communicate with so many different departments to get approvals, legal guidance, finance support, clear engineering dependencies, etc. so the value of replacing any of these communication requirements with AI interactions or API calls is huge. I touched on a similar topic with the [introduction of microservices](https://bmak.substack.com/p/an-unconventional-path-to-innovation) and this is a very similar concept.

**The new job landscape**

If we agree on productivity gains, what would be other consequences? Will engineers and other tech professionals lose their jobs? One theory is to assume that engineering will take the path farming has taken with technology evolution, where between 1900 and 2010, farmers have [decreased](https://www.nass.usda.gov/AgCensus/) from 7.5% to 0.7% of the US population. However, I think something else is more likely to happen. Byrne Hobart from [the Diff](https://www.thediff.co/archive/working-with-a-copilot/) alludes to the idea that automation doesn’t always mean that we decrease costs.

> *So one way to frame the task is to ask a two-part question:*
>
> *Which things you do can be automated? (And note that one part of automation is setting up things like scripts to download your emails and perform some batched action on some of them—not only is there more you can do with data, but the overhead of getting the data has gone down.)*
>
> *Which automatable components of a job are the most important complements to other work? There are some forms of automation that just decrease the time cost of certain kinds of overhead, like converting notes into bullet points or converting bullet points into prose. **In other cases, though, the best thing to do with a 90% cost reduction is to 10x the output instead.***

While a lot of mundane tasks like writing emails or organizing files will be mostly automated and take 100x less time and cost, we won’t ask for more of them. However, there are other tasks that we would do much more of the same given the option to do so. Packy McCormick elaborates on the idea in his latest “not boring” post [Intelligence Superabundance](https://www.notboring.co/p/intelligence-superabundance).

The main argument is that there is latent & induced demand for intelligence. Latent demand is demand that consumers cannot satisfy or do not know they need, which can be caused by lack of money, availability, information or the inability of the system to handle it. Induced demand is a phenomenon where increase in supply leads to a decline in price and an increase in consumption. If the supply of intelligence increases at a lower cost, we will consume more.

This doesn’t seem like a radical idea. The same has happened with several technological shifts that made some technology more accessible. Storage costs have [decreased](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/historical-cost-of-computer-memory-and-storage?country=~OWID_WRL) from $31.4m/TB in 1986 to $14.30/TB in 2022. The reason we are now taking thousands of photos or uploading millions of cat videos has to do with this cost optimization. While there are many debates about alignment and our ability to control AI or be taken over by robots, a future with abundant superintelligence is mostly exciting.

**A tool for discovery**

I will close with some optimistic thoughts from [Ben Hunt](https://www.epsilontheory.com/an-ai-in-the-city-of-god/) of Epsilon theory. Ben is excited about generative AI as this gives new possibilities to humanity.

> *Because generative AI is not just a tool of search, but a tool of discovery. Today we equate the idea of search with Google and the idea of discovery with Netflix Recommends or Amazon Suggested For You. My friends, that is the saddest sentence I have ever written.*
>
> *The great gift of generative AI like ChatGPT is that it takes the ideas of search and discovery away from the petty Tech Principalities and returns them into the individual hands of those with faith in the Spirit of Man. And that’s the most hopeful sentence I have ever written.*

And that’s one of the most hopeful sentences I have read recently. Combining human creativity with abundant superintelligence, the possibilities are endless.
