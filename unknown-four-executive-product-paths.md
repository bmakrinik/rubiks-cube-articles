# Four Executive Product Paths

I've been reflecting on the trajectory of careers within product management, focusing particularly on the paths available once a product leader gets to the director or executive levels. I am trying to avoid using terms like "ladders" as it suggests a degree of linearity and determinism that doesn't capture the essence of career progression.

For a product manager at the outset of their career, the roadmap (pun intended) seems relatively straightforward. Seniority is measured on three key dimensions that product managers acquire as they get to higher levels:

- **Expanding Scope:** This involves transitioning from overseeing a single feature or a small product to managing larger products and eventually a portfolio of products.
- **Navigating Ambiguity:** Early stages require implementing well-defined solutions, which evolves into defining solutions and ultimately identifying the right problem to solve.
- **Independence:** Progression sees a shift from frequent check-ins and guided management to a more autonomous mode of operation.

As product managers progress, their career options diversify, broadening the landscape of potential paths.

**Path 1: Chief Product Officer (CPO)**

A CPO is responsible for the product strategy and execution across the whole company. This route might also include responsibilities beyond product management, including overseeing engineering, data/analytics/science, and/or design teams.

**Path 2: General Manager (GM)**

There are many companies (e.g., big tech) where the product domain is too big to be centralized. For example, Amazon has products across e-commerce, AWS, Devices, Advertising among other domains, so there is no single CPO to oversee all of them (they converge at the SVP or CEO level). In this environment, the product GM role becomes pivotal. GMs oversee entire products or business lines, including product, technology, design, and go-to-market strategies (sales, marketing). Some of these could be dotted lines, but the GM owns the direction and has a P&L responsibility. For example, Amazon has several GMs leading AWS services.

**Path 3: Chief Operating Officer (COO)**  
A product management background offers a wide range of experiences and insights into various organizational functions. This makes product leaders well-suited for broader operational roles outside of the narrow product domain. For instance, Asha Sharma transitioned from VP of Product at Meta to COO at Instacart, spearheading efforts towards IPO and profitability while managing a $30 billion+ P&L.

**Path 4: Chief Executive Officer (CEO)**  
  
In product driven organizations, product experience is required for the continuous success of a company. Therefore, the head of the product org can be a primary candidate for the CEO position when such a transition happens. Ryan Roslansky's journey from CPO to CEO of LinkedIn over 11 years exemplifies this path, highlighting the critical role product leadership plays in sustaining organizational success.
